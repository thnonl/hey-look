The tour

Nine things you would usually rent. Try them.

This window is drawn for this page, with made-up mail. The buttons and keys really work. Scroll, and it changes scene.

  1. 01TriageOften a paid extra elsewhere

    Inbox zero at typing speed.

    J and K move, E archives, # deletes, S snoozes, ! sends to junk. No mouse, no menus. Archive, delete and junk reach your server only after 3 to 30 seconds (6 by default), so a slip is one click from undone.

    Try it. Click the window, then press J K E. Or use the buttons.

    Real keys: c, /, j, k, e, #, !, s, p, *, u, r, a, f.

  2. 02Undo sendOften a paid extra elsewhere

    A second chance after Send.

    The message waits in a local outbox for 0 to 60 seconds (10 by default). Hit Send, spot the typo, take it back.

    Try it. Press Send, then watch the countdown. Pick a window length.

    One honest catch: held mail survives a crash, but a normal Quit sends it at once.

  3. 03Send laterOften a paid extra elsewhere

    Write now. Land later.

    Pick a preset or any date and time, up to a year ahead. A worker inside the app sends it, so Hey Look! has to be running at that time.

    Try it. Press Send later and choose a time.

    If the app was closed, the message goes out at the next start (or waits as Overdue if you set it to ask). Temporary errors are retried up to 3 times.

  4. 04SnoozeOften a paid extra elsewhere

    Out of sight until it matters.

    Snooze a conversation to a preset (in 1 hour, later today, tomorrow, the weekend, next week) or to times you set yourself. It returns to the Inbox then.

    Try it. Press Snooze, choose a time, then jump ahead.

    A notification announces the return on Windows and Linux, not on macOS yet.

  5. 05Command barOften a paid extra elsewhere

    Press Ctrl+K. Do anything.

    Type a command or a folder name: new mail, go to the calendar, switch mailbox, sweep by sender, theme, archive, flag, pin. About 40 of them, all a few keystrokes away.

    Try it. Type dark, pane or snooz. Arrow keys and Enter work.

    Cmd+K on macOS. On this page, Ctrl+K jumps here.

  6. 06EncryptionOften a paid extra elsewhere

    Lock it. Sign it.

    OpenPGP is built in. Make an Ed25519 or RSA 3072 key, back it up, and see before you send whether each recipient has a usable key.

    Try it. Switch on Encrypt, then Sign.

    Secret keys live in the app's database, locked by your passphrase, not in the OS credential store. Not yet tested against other OpenPGP software.

  7. 07CalendarOften a paid extra elsewhere

    An invitation arrives. Answer it.

    Accept, tentative or decline from the card in the message. The reply goes out from the right mailbox, with an optional note. Month, week and agenda views.

    Try it. Press Accept.

    You can sync the calendars of a Google or Microsoft mailbox (optional, per mailbox). Google sign-in and Google calendar sync are limited to the first 100 users until Google finishes its review. CalDAV may come in a later update.

  8. 08Remote imagesOften a paid extra elsewhere

    Newsletters cannot phone home.

    Remote and background images stay blocked until you load them, once for a message or always for a mailbox. A tracking pixel is just a remote image, so it is blocked with the rest.

    Try it. Press Load content, then Block again.

    When you allow images, the app downloads them itself where it can. For any it cannot, the message view may request them directly.

  9. 09Privacy

    From here to there. That is the whole route.

    There is no Hey Look! server in the middle. Mail travels between the app and your provider. Passwords and sign-in tokens go to your system's credential store. There is no analytics code in the source.

    Try it. Watch the envelopes. Nothing goes upstairs.

    The only other trips are listed under the diagram. The "no analytics" line is verified from the source, not from a traffic capture.

    The honest ledger
    
      
    Where it is partial, and what is not there yet.
    
      
    For the current version. We would rather you read this here than find out later.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Partial
      
        
    • Calendar: events, invitations, reminders and three views, with optional sync for Google and Microsoft calendars. Google sign-in and Google calendar sync are limited to the first 100 users until Google finishes its review
    • Push mail: new Inbox mail arrives at once on servers that support IMAP IDLE; other folders and servers without it are checked at your interval (every 2 minutes by default)
    • Languages: English by default, Vietnamese and French in Settings. The translations are new and may contain mistakes
    • Appearance: light, dark, 7 colours or your own, 3 densities, 2 pane positions, 6 text sizes. No full theme editor
    • Updates: full on Windows; macOS and Linux need your permission
    • No analytics code in the source (verified from the code, not from a traffic capture)
    
      
    Not yet
      
        
    • No mobile apps. Desktop only.
    • Installers are not code-signed. Expect a SmartScreen or Gatekeeper warning on first start.
    • macOS is not notarised and has no native notifications yet; the Dock icon does show the unread number.
    • CalDAV and other calendar services. Calendar sync covers Google and Microsoft only; CalDAV may come in a later update.
    • Search covers downloaded mail only. Not the server.
    • More languages than English, Vietnamese and French may come in a later update.
