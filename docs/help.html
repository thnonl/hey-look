Install and first start

Windows warns before the first start

The installers are not code-signed yet. If SmartScreen appears, choose More info, then Run anyway. Use the .exe; the .msi is for managed installs.

macOS does not open the app the first time

The app is not notarised by Apple, so macOS blocks its first start once. Follow the three steps on the download page; updates start normally after that.

Linux: which file, and what it needs

The AppImage runs on any distro; the .deb is for Debian and Ubuntu, the .rpm for Fedora and openSUSE. Passwords need GNOME Keyring or KWallet running and unlocked, and links open with xdg-open.

Accounts and mailboxes

Add an account

Sign in with Google or Microsoft on the provider’s own page, or add any other account with IMAP and SMTP: Hey Look! finds the servers from your address. iCloud and Yahoo need an app password from your account page. Some Microsoft work and school accounts need an admin to allow IMAP and SMTP.

Google says “Google hasn’t verified this app”, or Sign in with Google stops working

Hey Look! has not finished Google’s review yet. Google shows this warning for every app under review; nothing is wrong with your account.

To continue, choose Advanced, then Go to Hey Look! (unsafe), and allow the permissions if you agree with them (see Google API data use).

Until the review is done, Google lets only the first 100 people sign in with Google, for mail and for calendar sync. If sign-in stops working, add a personal Gmail account with an app password instead, as an IMAP account with Gmail as the provider.

Does new mail arrive at once?

Yes, for the Inbox, on servers that support push (IMAP IDLE), while Hey Look! is running. Other folders, and servers without push, are checked every 2 minutes by default.

Everyday use

Keyboard shortcuts

Ctrl K (Cmd K on macOS) opens the command bar. In the mail list: c new mail, / search, j and k move, e archive, # delete, s snooze, r reply.

Undo send and send later

After Send, a message waits 10 seconds by default (up to 60) so you can undo it; a normal Quit sends it at once. Send later needs Hey Look! to be running at that time; a message that was due while it was closed goes out at the next start by default.

Updates

Hey Look! checks GitHub when it starts and, at most every 2 hours, when its window comes back; after a failed check it tries again in 15 minutes. You can turn this off in Settings. Nothing is downloaded until you agree, and only a correctly signed update is installed. The .msi asks for administrator rights, the .deb and .rpm for your password.

If you installed Hey Look! from the Microsoft Store, the Store keeps it up to date, and Settings > About says so.

The Store version keeps its own mail, accounts and settings, apart from a copy installed from this website. Use one of them on a computer, not both.

Change the language

Choose it in Settings, under General. English is the default; Hey Look! does not follow the language of your system.

Privacy and where your data lives

Where is my data?

Mail and settings are in one local folder: %APPDATA%\thnonl.heylook on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/thnonl.heylook on macOS, ~/.local/share/thnonl.heylook on Linux. Passwords and tokens are in your system’s credential store; OpenPGP secret keys are in the app’s database, locked by your passphrase.

What leaves my computer?

Only what you use: your mail servers, Google or Microsoft sign-in and calendar, the server lookup when you add an account, GitHub for updates, your AI endpoint, images you allow and unsubscribe requests. There is no analytics code. The privacy policy lists every connection.

Uninstall, and remove everything

Remove your accounts in Settings (your mail stays on the server), uninstall the app, then delete its data folder and the entries whose names start with heylook in your system’s credential store.

OpenPGP basics

Make a key, encrypt, sign

Create a key in Settings, protected by a passphrase, and save the backup it offers. When you write, switch on Encrypt or Sign; Hey Look! shows whether each recipient has a key. Not yet tested with other OpenPGP software.

Report a problem

A bug or a feature idea

Open an issue on GitHub and say which system and Hey Look! version you use (shown in Settings, under About). Issues are public, so never post private mail, passwords or tokens.

A security problem

Report it privately: open github.com/thnonl/hey-look/security and choose “Report a vulnerability”, or write to namth.onl@gmail.com. Please do not open a public issue. See Security.

Content from the AI Assistant

The AI Assistant sends your request to the AI service you set up in Settings > AI Assistant, and that service writes the answer. Hey Look! does not provide or control the AI model, and text written by AI can be wrong.

If the AI Assistant shows something harmful, offensive or illegal, tell us: open an issue on GitHub or write to namth.onl@gmail.com. Name the AI service and the model, and never include private mail or your API key. Please report it to your AI service as well.

Known limitations

What Hey Look! does not do yet

The installers are not code-signed, Google sign-in is limited to 100 people for now, search covers downloaded mail only, and calendar sync covers Google and Microsoft only. The full list is on the Features page.
