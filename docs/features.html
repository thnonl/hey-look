01

Mailboxes and sync

Bring every account. Mail comes straight from your providers.

  • Google sign-in

    Sign in with your Google account. Hey Look! has not finished Google’s review yet, so Google shows a warning first and allows up to 100 people to sign in. What to do

    Google review pending

  • Microsoft sign-in (OAuth)

    Outlook.com and Microsoft 365 through Microsoft's own window (public client, PKCE, no secret).

    Work accounts may block IMAP/SMTP

  • iCloud, Yahoo and any IMAP/SMTP account

    Add iCloud or Yahoo with an app password, or any other account that offers IMAP and SMTP.

  • Detect settings

    Type an address and the app finds the servers: presets, provider autoconfig, the Thunderbird ISPDB, DNS and probing.

  • Trust a certificate for one account

    For servers with their own certificate, such as Proton Mail Bridge on 127.0.0.1. It turns certificate checks off for that account only.

    Off by default

  • Combined inbox or one mailbox

    See all mailboxes in one list, or switch to a single mailbox from the folder pane or the command bar.

  • Folders and quick views

    Standard and custom folders with nesting, plus quick views: Unread, Flagged, Pinned, Snoozed, Scheduled.

  • Download limit per mailbox

    Choose how many recent mails to keep on this computer: 500, 1000, 2000 or 5000 (default 2000). Older mail stays on the server.

  • Offline reading and search

    Mail you have already downloaded can be read and searched without a connection.

  • New mail at once (push)

    New Inbox mail arrives at once on servers that support IMAP IDLE. A switch in Settings > Mailboxes > Reading & sync turns it off. A server without IDLE, or a lost connection, falls back to checking at your interval (every 2 minutes by default). While push works, the regular check of all folders runs only every 10 minutes (or your interval, if it is longer).

    Inbox only

  • Order and Display name

    Drag mailboxes (or use Alt+Up and Alt+Down) into the order you like, and give each a Display name that is shown instead of the address.

02

Reading

A quiet reading pane that does not let a message run wild.

  • Conversations

    Messages of one thread are grouped, and you act on the whole conversation.

  • Focused and Other

    Newsletters and bulk mail (anything with List-Unsubscribe) move to an Other tab.

    Header heuristic

  • Remote content blocked by default

    Remote and background images stay blocked until you load them, once for a message or always for a mailbox.

  • Tracking pixels

    Because every remote image is blocked, a tracking pixel is blocked with them. The app keeps no tracker list and does not claim to detect trackers.

  • Images you allow

    The app downloads them itself (up to 60 per message, 6 MB each). Where it cannot, the message view may request an image directly.

  • Sandboxed message frame

    Each message is shown without scripts, under a restrictive content policy, with no iframe, object, embed or form.

  • Message background

    Mail is shown on the background its sender chose, also in the dark theme. A button in the reading pane switches a message between that original background and a dark one; your choice is remembered.

  • Link text warning

    If the visible text of a link is a web address that points to a different host, you get a "This link may not go where it says" confirmation.

    URL-shaped link text only

  • Risky attachment warning

    Opening an executable or script attachment (exe, msi, bat, ps1, js, lnk and more) asks first.

    On by default

  • One-click unsubscribe

    For newsletters, unsubscribe with the RFC 8058 one-click request, or open the sender's page or a mailto.

  • Save attachments

    Save one attachment or all of them to a folder. Inline pictures appear in the message.

  • Print and view original

    Print the open message (not offered on macOS) and show the raw message source.

  • Read in a window of its own

    Double-click a conversation (or press Shift+Enter) to read it in a window with Reply, Forward, Archive, Delete, Flag, Snooze, Summarise and Translate. Up to 8 windows can be open.

  • PDF attachments

    On Windows the preview draws the PDF inside the app. On macOS and Linux it offers Open in your default app and Save as.

03

Writing

Compose with a safety net.

  • Rich-text editor

    Font and size, colours and highlight, lists, alignment, styles, text direction, tables, pictures, links, emoji and symbols, find and replace, and clear formatting, in one row of buttons. The message is sent as plain inline formatting that other mail programs show the same way.

  • Paste options

    Pasting from Word, Excel, Outlook, web pages and other apps is cleaned. A small button after the paste switches it between keeping the source formatting, merging into the text around the cursor, and text only. Ctrl+Shift+V (Cmd+Shift+V on a Mac) always pastes text only.

  • Replies keep the look of the original

    The quoted message sits in a block of its own, below your text and signature, with its fonts, colours, tables, lists and pictures. The block cannot be broken by accident.

  • Write in a window of its own

    Hold Shift when you click New mail, Reply or Forward, double-click a draft, or press Pop out (Ctrl+Shift+O). A setting makes a window the normal way. Each window remembers its size and place; up to 8 can be open.

  • Autosave and restore

    The message is saved to Drafts by itself about every 30 seconds, and a copy is kept on your computer every few seconds. If the app ends unexpectedly, the next start saves it to Drafts by itself and says so in a toast. A message with Encrypt on is not copied to the disk.

  • Importance and preview

    Mark a message High or Low importance; mail you receive shows it in the list. Preview as recipient shows the message on a light or a dark background and changes nothing in what is sent.

  • Confidential

    A message marked Confidential is shown without copy, print or forwarding, and its window is hidden from screen capture and recording. Linux does not block screen capture.

    Windows, macOS

  • Recipient chips

    Addresses are checked as chips, with suggestions from people you wrote to and who wrote to you. Cc and Bcc included.

  • Attach by button, drag or paste

    Drop on the top of the composer to put a picture in the message, on the bottom to attach it.

  • 25 MB size warning

    Warns when attachments plus pictures pass the usual server limit and blocks the send until you reduce them.

  • Signatures and templates

    Create them for all accounts or one mailbox and insert a template into the body.

  • Undo send

    After Send the message waits in a local outbox for 0, 5, 10, 20, 30 or 60 seconds (default 10). It survives a crash or kill. A normal Quit sends it at once.

    Quit sends at once

  • Send later

    Pick a preset or any date and time up to one year ahead. A worker inside the app sends it, so the app has to be running.

    App must be running

  • If the app was closed

    A message whose time passed while the app was closed is sent at the next start by default. The "ask" setting makes it wait as Overdue instead.

  • Retries for scheduled sends

    Temporary network and server errors are retried up to 3 times, 5 minutes apart. Login and address errors are not.

  • Meeting from the composer

    A Meeting switch turns a message into a calendar invitation with an event in your calendar.

  • Ctrl+Enter sends

    Cmd+Enter on macOS.

04

Organising

Keep the inbox short without a mouse.

  • Undo for archive, delete, junk

    These reach the server after 3, 6, 10, 15 or 30 seconds (default 6), so you can take them back.

  • Snooze with your own times

    Presets (in 1 hour, later today, tomorrow, weekend, next week) or times you set. The conversation returns to the Inbox then.

  • Snooze reminders

    A notification when a snoozed conversation returns.

    Windows, Linux

  • Pin, flag, read and unread

    Pin to the top, flag, and mark read or unread from keys or the command bar.

  • Mark as read timing

    Immediately, after 2 or 5 seconds, or never.

  • Block sender

    Moves the conversation to Junk and hides later mail from that address in the Inbox view. Nothing is moved on the server.

  • Filters

    Rules on From, To, Subject or any field ("contains" or "equals") that archive, delete, junk, mark read, flag, pin or move to a folder. They run on newly synced mail.

  • Sweep

    A dialog lists your senders. Per sender: archive all, delete, mark read or block.

  • Bulk selection

    Ctrl+A selects all, then act on the selection.

  • Right-click menu

    Right-click a conversation (or press Shift+F10) for Reply, Forward, Flag, Pin, Snooze, Move to, Archive, Delete, Move to Junk and more. With several ticked, it acts on all of them.

05

Search

Operators you already know.

  • Search operators

    from:, to:, subject:, body:, folder:, is:unread|read|flagged|pinned, has:attachment, after:, before: (dates or 7d) and "quoted phrases".

  • Search panel

    Scope (this folder, a mailbox, everything), filters and date pickers.

  • Search in webmail

    For a mailbox whose webmail is known, a button opens it. Gmail gets the same search with the operators translated.

06

Calendar

Works on your computer, with optional sync for Google and Microsoft.

  • Month, Week and Agenda views

    Switch with Ctrl+1 and Ctrl+2, or the m, w and a keys.

  • Invitations with Accept, Tentative, Decline

    A card shows the time in your zone, place, organiser, attendees and conflicts. The reply goes out from the right account, with an optional note.

  • Updates and cancellations

    Recognised when the From address equals the stored organiser. There is no DKIM, SPF or S/MIME check.

  • Reminders

    Event reminders fire from a background worker.

  • Import and export .ics

    Bring a calendar file in, send events out.

  • Time zones and first day

    Times show in your zone (automatic by default). Weeks start on Monday, Sunday or Saturday.

  • Repeating events

    Shown as a read-only series. A single occurrence cannot be edited.

    Limit

  • Sync with Google and Microsoft

    Turn it on per mailbox in Settings > Mailboxes > (account) > Calendar. The calendars you choose appear next to your local calendar with their colours, and the events you add, change, delete or answer are saved back to Google Calendar or Outlook. Calendars you can only read, such as holidays, stay read-only. It is a separate permission from mail, off until you turn it on, and turning it off removes the synced calendars from this computer.

    Optional

  • Google calendar sync and Google’s review

    Hey Look! has not finished Google’s review yet. Until it has, Google shows a warning first and allows up to 100 people to sign in with Google, for mail and for calendar. See what to do. Microsoft calendar sync is not affected; a work or school account may need an admin to approve it.

    Limit

  • CalDAV and other calendar services

    Not supported. They may come in a later update.

    Not yet

07

Encryption (OpenPGP)

Built in, with the details spelled out.

  • Key generation

    Create an Ed25519 / Curve25519 or RSA 3072 key with an optional expiry, locked by a passphrase.

  • Encrypt, sign, decrypt, verify

    Encrypt a message to the recipients' keys, sign it, decrypt incoming mail and verify signatures.

    Not yet tested with other OpenPGP software

  • Import, export, backup

    Import and export armored keys and save a passphrase-protected backup file.

  • Backup guard

    Closing the key page without a backup asks "Back up now" or "Close anyway".

  • Passphrase attempts limit

    After repeated wrong passphrases, unlocking waits 30 seconds.

  • Recipient key status

    Shows whether each recipient has a usable key before you send.

  • Where secret keys live

    In the app's own database, locked with your passphrase. They are not in the OS credential store.

08

AI Assistant (optional)

Off until you point it at an endpoint of your choice.

  • Summarise a conversation

    2 to 5 bullet points for a thread. Sends up to 24,000 characters of the readable messages.

    Needs your endpoint

  • Translate a message

    Into a language you pick. Sends up to 60,000 characters, in parts of about 8,000 characters one after the other. A notice shows the part it is on and a Cancel button; a part that fails can be retried without redoing the finished ones.

    Needs your endpoint

  • Write with AI

    Writes or edits the body from your instruction. Sends your draft, the subject and, for replies, up to 6,000 characters of the message you reply to.

    Needs your endpoint

  • Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint

    Works with any OpenAI-style /v1/chat/completions endpoint, including local servers. The model list comes from /models.

  • Key in the OS credential store

    The AI key is kept in the system credential store, not in the database.

  • What leaves the machine

    The text you ask about goes to the endpoint you configured. Decrypted OpenPGP content is sent too, because the assistant reads the readable messages. The endpoint's own terms and privacy policy apply to what it receives.

  • Help when it cannot work

    If the Assistant is not set up, the key is refused, the address or model is wrong, the provider is busy or the answer is too slow, a message stays on screen until you close it and says what happened. Its button opens Settings > AI Assistant at the right field. Summarise and Write with AI can be cancelled.

Notifications, tray and window

The gaps of each platform are spelled out.

  • Notifications with buttons

    New mail on Windows shows up to three action buttons: Archive, Delete, Mark as read by default, or Flag, Reply, Snooze.

    Windows

  • Notifications on Linux

    A plain native notification through the desktop's notification service, without buttons.

    Linux

  • macOS notifications

    Not available in this version. The Settings page says so.

    macOSLimit

  • Smart notifications

    Bulk mail does not notify when smart mode is on (default).

    Windows, Linux

  • Tray icon and unread badge

    The unread count, a dot, or nothing (setting), plus a Windows taskbar overlay. On macOS the Dock icon shows the unread number (or nothing, by the same setting).

    Windows, macOS Dock

  • Tray menu

    Open Hey Look!, New mail, Check mail now, Pause notifications, Quit.

  • Keep running in the tray

    Closing the window keeps the app and its sync running. Turn it off and the close button quits.

  • Windows title bar

    On Windows the top bar is the title bar, with Snap Layouts on Windows 11 and F11 for full screen.

    Windows

10

Updates

Signed, and only after you agree.

  • In-app update

    The app checks GitHub for a newer version and downloads nothing until you agree. It then downloads it with a progress bar, verifies the minisign signature and restarts into it after a 5-second countdown you can cancel. The Windows .msi asks for administrator rights, and the Linux .deb and .rpm ask for your password.

    Asks first on every system

  • Update addresses are checked

    Only https://github.com/thnonl/hey-look/releases/download/<tag>/<file> is accepted.

11

Keyboard, command bar and looks

Everything you reach by mouse has a key or a command.

  • Mail shortcuts

    c new, / search, j and k move, e archive, # or Delete delete, ! junk, s snooze, p pin, * flag, u unread, r a f reply, reply all, forward, Esc close, Ctrl+A select all, F5 sync, F11 full screen. Backspace deletes on macOS only.

  • Calendar shortcuts

    c new event, t today, m w a for month, week, agenda, arrows for previous and next.

  • Command bar

    Ctrl+K (Cmd+K): new mail, sync, new event, calendar, settings, updates, Sweep, theme, reading pane position, conversations, Focused, switch account, archive, delete, flag, pin and any folder.

  • Appearance

    Light, dark or system; 7 colour presets or any colour; 3 densities; reading pane right or bottom; 4 list switches; 6 text sizes. Try it on the home page.

  • Languages

    English by default; Vietnamese, French, Chinese (Simplified), Spanish, Japanese, German and Russian can be chosen in Settings > General and apply at once, without a restart. Dates and numbers follow the language. The translations were written with AI help and checked by language specialists.

    English, Vietnamese, French, Chinese, Spanish, Japanese, German, Russian

  • Accessibility

    Keyboard shortcuts and ARIA labels on controls. No accessibility audit or conformance claim.

    Partial

  • Side menu

    Fold the folder pane to a column of icons with the button at its top or Ctrl+B (Cmd+B). In a window narrower than 1100 px it folds by itself, and pointing at the icons opens the full menu over the list for a moment. Settings > Appearance sets Automatic, Always expanded or Always icons only.

  • Shortcuts while writing

    Ctrl+K link, Ctrl+F find, Ctrl+H replace, Shift+F3 change case, Ctrl+Shift+V paste as text only, Ctrl+Shift+O pop out, Alt+F10 the toolbar (Cmd instead of Ctrl on a Mac).

12

Data, privacy and platform

Your mail stays between you and your provider.

  • Local storage

    The mail cache and settings are a local SQLite file under the identifier thnonl.heylook.

  • Secrets in the credential store

    Passwords, OAuth tokens and the AI key live in Windows Credential Manager, the macOS Keychain or the Linux Secret Service. OpenPGP secret keys are the exception.

    Linux needs an unlocked keyring

  • No account, no licence check

    No Hey Look! account and no licence server. There is no sign-in to the app itself.

  • No analytics code

    No analytics or crash-reporting SDK is in the source. That is verified from the source, not by a traffic capture.

  • No ads

    The app shows no advertising and has no ad code in the source. That is verified from the source, not by a traffic capture. The only message of that kind is an occasional reminder, about once every two months, that you can support the project; Settings > General > Occasional support reminder turns it off.

  • Every outbound request

    Your mail servers; Google and Microsoft sign-in; Google Calendar and Microsoft Graph, only for a mailbox with calendar sync on; autoconfig, ISPDB and DNS for Detect settings; GitHub for updates; the AI endpoint you configure; the sender's unsubscribe address when you press Unsubscribe; remote images only after you allow them.

  • Quit safety

    Held sends and queued actions are stored locally. A message that was being sent when the app stopped is queued again at the next start.

  • Windows, macOS and Linux

    Windows 10/11, macOS 11+ (universal, not notarised), Linux (AppImage, .deb, .rpm).

!

Limits, in plain words

What Hey Look! does not do, or does only in part.

  • Platforms

    On macOS the Dock icon shows the unread number but there are no notifications, the app is signed with the project’s own certificate and is not notarised, first start needs the steps on the download page, and the Print menu is absent. On Linux notifications have no buttons, links need xdg-open or a similar opener, the tray icon has no click action (use Open Hey Look! in its menu), and a Confidential message is not hidden from screen capture.

  • Installers

    Not code-signed (SmartScreen on Windows, Gatekeeper on macOS).

  • Mail delivery

    Push (IMAP IDLE) covers the Inbox of servers that support it; other servers are checked at your interval (every 2 minutes by default). Servers must offer TLS or STARTTLS.

  • Search

    Covers downloaded mail only.

  • Calendar

    Calendar sync covers Google and Microsoft; CalDAV and other services may come in a later update. Google sign-in and Google calendar sync are limited to the first 100 users until Google finishes its review. A single occurrence of a repeating event cannot be edited.

  • Folders

    Missing standard folders (Drafts, Sent, Archive, Deleted Items) are not created. Actions that need them show an error.

  • Drafts

    A message you write is saved to Drafts about every 30 seconds and a copy is kept on your computer. If the app is closed by force, or the Mac logs out, the next start saves it to Drafts by itself and says so in a toast; the text of the last few seconds may be missing, and a message with Encrypt on is not copied to the disk.

  • Overlay tools

    Overlay tools such as RivaTuner can crash the web view. The app restarts itself up to three times in ten minutes.

  • Language

    English by default, with Vietnamese, French, Chinese (Simplified), Spanish, Japanese, German and Russian in Settings. The translations were written with AI help and checked by language specialists.

  • Sign-in

    Work and school accounts (Microsoft calls each organisation a “tenant”) may block IMAP/SMTP until an admin enables it.

  • Not tested on the release build

    Windows tray Quit during an undo window, the installer over a running app, opening links and attachments on Windows; macOS first-start steps and logging out with the app running.

  • PDF files

    PDF files are drawn inside the app on Windows only. On macOS and Linux they open in your default app.

  • Writing

    There is no plain-text-only mode. Format Painter, AutoFormat and the mini toolbar are not there yet.
